# vid2grid — notes for agents

See README.md for what this is and how the pipeline fits together.

- Pure client-side app: tracking (MediaPipe) runs in the browser; there is no API route or DB. Keep it that way unless a server model is explicitly wanted.
- `lib/` is the engine and has no React in it; `components/` is UI. `components/App.tsx` owns all state; the Score is *derived* (`useMemo`) from tracked extractions + grid/smooth/lift settings, so changing a setting re-snaps without re-tracking.
- Frames/conventions (body-local az/el, stage frame, facing) are documented at the top of `lib/geometry.ts`, `lib/pose.ts`, `lib/fk.ts`. Body-local is left-handed (x right, y up, z forward); the stage/three.js frame is right-handed. Don't derive "right" from cross products without checking `lib/fk.ts` (R = F × U).
- `public/mediapipe` and `public/models` are generated by `scripts/setup-assets.mjs` (postinstall) and gitignored.
- The stage's Smooth/Stepped toggle (`motion` in `App.tsx`, threaded to `Stage`) only picks which track the *figure* draws — `score.raw` vs `score.frames`. Never let it reach the notation, the score, or an export: the snapped track stays the truth. The cast samples the same track, or the stage disagrees with itself.
- Cast members carry choreographic devices (`lib/devices.ts`: delay / rate / mirror / reverse) and are sampled per frame via `clipTime` + `placePose`; the stage clock runs to `memberSpan`. Figures inside the video (`lib/invideo.ts`, `components/VideoStage.tsx`) are an orthographic scene exactly `metresPerUnit` wide laid over the letterboxed picture, anchored on the person's tracked hips and a smoothed floor — no camera calibration; poses arrive already in picture metres. While the `<video>` is playing, its `currentTime` is the stage clock (figures must not drift from the picture).
- Unit tests: `npm test` (node:test via tsx, `test/*.test.ts`). `lib/tempo.ts` estimates a pulse by autocorrelation — it must find a *prominent interior peak*, not just a high correlation, or smooth unpulsed clips get reported as the top of the BPM range at high confidence (there's a regression test for exactly that).
- Typecheck with `npx tsc --noEmit -p .` (the `LayoutProps` error is a Next-generated global; ignore until `next dev`/`build` has run). Lint with `npx eslint .`.
- The dev server may already be running on :3000 (`.next/dev/logs/next-development.log` has browser + server logs). Headless e2e: a Playwright script that uploads a clip and waits for "keyframes" text works; the sample clip used in development was a 10 s excerpt of Wikimedia Commons "File:Rounds in Kathak.webm" (CC, 1080p, 30 s), which is close up — shrink it into a corner of a 1920×1080 frame with ffmpeg `scale=384:216,pad=1920:1080:1100:500` to exercise the crop.
- Crop (`lib/crop.ts`, fractions of the frame) is chosen in the clip preview (`components/CropEditor.tsx`) and is *the* frame from then on: `trackVideo` paints it into a canvas for MediaPipe, image landmarks are normalised to it, `SourceInfo.width/height` are its pixel size and `SourceInfo.crop` records it. `VideoPane` and the Objects plate map the crop into their box; nothing downstream needs to know about the full frame. A full-frame crop is stored as absent.
- The tracker asks MediaPipe for up to `MAX_PEOPLE` poses per frame and `lib/follow.ts` decides who the dancer is afterwards (`chooseDancer` in `lib/tracker.ts`): the person under the user's pick (`PersonPick`, full-frame fractions + time, chosen in `CropEditor`), followed both ways from that moment, or the biggest body with re-seeding when lost. MediaPipe's pose order is *not* stable between frames, so never index `landmarks[0]` and call it the dancer. Keep follow.ts pure; `test/follow.test.ts` covers crossings, loss, and re-acquisition.
- MediaPipe's world (3D) depth is unreliable; `lib/lift.ts` keeps image xy exact and clamps depth by bone length. Smoothing must stay fast (`beta` ≈ 1) or fast arm sweeps lag by several frames.
- Live mode (`lib/live.ts` engine, `lib/capture.ts` camera loop, `LiveBar`): the causal twin of the offline pipeline — `CausalSnapper` (dwell, no back-dating), `PoseSmoother` (shared with `smoothPoses`), a body re-measured on a growing sample. The stage draws `LiveFrame`s straight from the capture's `onFrame`; `time` is the take's clock so the cast dances along. On Finish, `resampleLive` lays the irregular samples onto `SAMPLE_FPS` and the result becomes an ordinary `analysis` (plus the MediaRecorder file as `src`), so everything downstream is unchanged. The loop is paced by `requestAnimationFrame` + wall clock, *not* `requestVideoFrameCallback` (which never fires while the camera pane is `display:none` on the 3D stage), and the first inference is taken on a blank canvas in `open()` because it stalls for seconds and a stall freezes the camera feed. Headless e2e: Chrome with `--use-fake-device-for-media-stream --use-file-for-fake-video-capture=<clip>.y4m` (ffmpeg `-pix_fmt yuv420p` from the sample clip; loop it with `-stream_loop`, the capture ends with the file).
- The landing page (`components/Welcome.tsx`) is black and minimal on purpose; its hero (`components/HeroDuet.tsx`) is a real clip, `public/demo/alice.mp4`, with a VRM character dancing its score beside the picture. The score and per-frame picture anchors are `public/demo/alice.json`, generated by the app's own pipeline with `scripts/hero-data.ts` (bundle with esbuild, run in a browser on the dev server — see the file). Don't hand-edit that JSON; re-run the generator if the clip changes.
